    Two point cloud pipelines with one output, and the simulation's cameras carry the robot's names and frames

    On Foxy the simulation's depth came from Gazebo Classic's own point cloud, which had "coordinate system problems" that were never resolved, and mapping was tried with a sim-only rtabmap_test.launch.py on the old /oak_d/* topics, frame oak-d_frame and an odom_raw odometry frame. On the Humble line no point cloud can be computed from the robot's data, because the Humble mjpeg_node publishes no camera_info. Milestone 2 (pilsbot_vscode_ws#35) makes the sim produce the robot's camera data on the robot's topic names and frames, in a city world, so that mapping and the Nav2 costmaps can be developed without the robot. Whether the OAK's on-device depth or host-side stereo works better can only be decided on the real OAK, so both point cloud pipelines exist, in the sim and on the robot, and both publish one output, /camera/points. Gazebo's own point cloud is not used.

    • Images: pilsbot_simulation bridges the five camera images with ros_gz_image's image_bridge (image_transport) instead of parameter_bridge, under the robot's names: /camera/color/image_raw, /camera/left/image_rect, /camera/right/image_rect, /camera/stereo/depth and /front_camera/image_raw. Each has one compressed transport beside it: /compressed on the four colour and mono images, /compressedDepth on depth. parameter_bridge (config/bridge.yaml) carries only /clock and the camera_infos (pilsbot_simulation#12).
    • Cameras off: the xacro argument cameras:=true|false (default true) leaves the five Gazebo <sensor>s out (pilsbot_description#11). It passes sim.launch.py → pilsbot_bringup's pilsbot.launch.py → pilsbot_driver's launch → xacro, next to hardware and use_sim_time (pilsbot_bringup#15, pilsbot_hw_ros#20). With false nothing is rendered, no camera topic exists, and image_bridge and the relay below are not started. The drive launch test uses it. With hardware:=real it changes nothing.
    • Stereo baseline relay: Fortress publishes half the OAK's baseline on every stereo camera (below). bridge.yaml puts the left, right and depth camera_info on /gz/camera/{left,right,stereo}/camera_info, and the sim-only scripts/camera_info_relay.py republishes them on the robot's names with left and depth P[3] = 0 and right P[3] = −fx·B. B comes from tf, i.e. from the URDF (0.075 m). It holds the right camera_info back until tf has the baseline and keeps the stamps, so exact-time sync still works.
    • Point cloud pipelines: pilsbot_navigation's perception.launch.py (cloud:=depth|stereo, default depth; use_sim_time; sync:=approximate|exact, default approximate) runs one composable container (pilsbot_navigation#4):
    • depth: depth_image_proc PointCloudXyzNode on /camera/stereo/depth + /camera/stereo/camera_info.
    • stereo: stereo_image_proc DisparityNode and PointCloudNode on /camera/{left,right}/image_rect + their camera_infos.
    • Both publish sensor_msgs/PointCloud2 on /camera/points in oak_left_camera_optical_frame. Only the QoS differs: depth publishes best effort, stereo reliable, so consumers subscribe with sensor-data QoS, which receives both. The same file runs on the robot and in the sim; nothing robot- or sim-specific is in it. approximate is the default for the real OAK; in the sim the pair is stamped identically and exact works too.
    • Costmaps: config/costmaps.yaml reads /camera/points in every observation source, with robot_base_frame: base_footprint and a footprint polygon from the robot profile and the URDF instead of robot_radius: 0.22. local_costmap.launch.py runs the standalone nav2_costmap_2d node for tests and Foxglove (pilsbot_navigation#5). Full Nav2 navigation is a later milestone.
    • Mapping: pilsbot_navigation's mapping.launch.py (input:=depth|stereo, default depth; use_sim_time; sync:=approximate|exact as in perception.launch.py; delete_db_on_start, default false; database_path, default ~/.ros/rtabmap.db) runs rtabmap on the OAK's images, not on /camera/points (pilsbot_navigation#6, resolves pilsbot_navigation#2):
    • depth: rtabmap_sync's rgbd_sync pairs /camera/left/image_rect + its camera_info with /camera/stereo/depth.
    • stereo: stereo_sync pairs /camera/{left,right}/image_rect + their camera_infos; rtabmap takes the baseline from the right P[3].
    • rtabmap_slam's rtabmap in namespace /rtabmap with frame_id: base_footprint, no visual odometry and Reg/Force3DoF. The wheel odometry is the controller's odom → base_footprint transform, read from tf at each pair's stamp (odom_frame_id: odom; no odom_raw), not the /odom topic, which would carry the controller's yaw variance of 1000 rad² into the graph. The spec says "odom topic"; the PR leaves this to its review.
    • rtabmap is the only publisher of map → odom. The occupancy grid is on /map (for the global costmap's static layer later), split by height as in costmaps.yaml (ground up to 0.1 m above base_footprint, obstacles up to 2 m) instead of rtabmap's normals segmentation, with a radius noise filter.
    • With delete_db_on_start:=false a new run continues the map in the database (the old rtabmap_test.launch.py deleted it on every start).

    pilsbot_simulation's rtabmap_test.launch.py is deleted (pilsbot_simulation#14), so until #6 is merged there is no rtabmap launch on humble. - camera_info on the robot (pending): mjpeg_node publishes a camera_info next to each image from the OAK's factory calibration, with the right camera's intrinsics on both rectified images, right P[3] = −fx·baseline, depth aligned to the left camera (RECTIFIED_LEFT, so frame, camera_info and the depth image agree) and capture-time stamps on all four streams (pilsbot_depthai#5). It is compiled and unit-tested only, and is merged only after its hardware test on the OAK. Until then neither pipeline runs on real data. - CSI camera: on the robot and in the sim its images are /front_camera/image_raw (+ /compressed) and /front_camera/camera_info, stamped with the new front_camera_optical_frame, a fixed child of front_camera_link with the REP 103 optical rotation (z forward, x right, y down) (pilsbot_bringup#12, pilsbot_bringup#16, pilsbot_description#11). The sim's sensor is 1280×720 at 30 fps with the robot's horizontal field of view and no distortion (below). - citysim: the Gazebo Classic city world is ported to Fortress on citysim's humble branch, as an ament package with an environment hook for the resource and plugin paths, its external models referenced by Fuel URI, and its plugins ported (below) (citysim#1 to #4). sim.launch.py takes world:=flat|citysim|citysim_block|<path>; flat stays the default for tests and control work, and both city worlds spawn the robot on a road at (−35, −1.8, 5.03) (pilsbot_simulation#14).

    Verified in the spike and the tickets

    The spike (pilsbot_simulation#4, branches spike/m2 in citysim, pilsbot_simulation and pilsbot_description, numbers in pilsbot_simulation/spike_evidence_m2/) ran on the laptop (i7-6500U, Intel HD 520) with ign-gazebo 6.18.0, ign-rendering 6.6.4, ros_gz 0.244.27 and gz_ros2_control 0.7.21, headless, on 2026-11-28. The tickets repeated the checks on the desktop.

    • Frame test, both pipelines: a textured target at a known pose in front of the robot; each cloud is transformed with tf into base_footprint at its stamp. In the spike (a box with its front face at x = 3.0) the depth cloud lay on the face exactly and the stereo cloud within 8.4 mm (p95), neither mirrored. The committed launch test in pilsbot_navigation uses a centred L-shaped target of two thin boards, so that a mirrored cloud puts the tower on the wrong side and an upside-down one loses the low half: depth 100 % of the points within 5 cm of the face, stereo 99.83 % (mean −6.8 mm, p95 17.5 mm), mirrored region empty for both. The tf base_footprint ← oak_left_camera_optical_frame has the correct optical rotation, so the OAK frames in the URDF stay as they are.
    • Gazebo's own point cloud: read in the frame its header names (oak_left_camera_optical_frame), 0 % of its points lay on the face, which appeared mirrored and in the wrong place (x 0.53…1.49, y −6.2…−0.6). Read in the x-forward link frame oak_left_camera_frame, 100 % did. The cloud is x-forward data under an optical-frame header, the likely cause of the Classic "coordinate system problems".
    • Half the baseline: Fortress publishes P[3] = −16.2866 = −fx·0.0375 on the left, the right and the depth camera_info (fx 434.31), half the 7.5 cm baseline and on the left camera too; the colour camera has 0. stereo_image_proc takes the baseline from the right P[3], so on the raw camera_infos it placed the box at half its distance from the camera (x = 1.906 instead of 3.0). With the relay it placed it at 2.992. depth_image_proc ignores P[3].
    • Images: image_bridge gives the robot's names with /compressed and costs the same real-time factor as parameter_bridge (0.229 vs 0.230). /camera/stereo/depth/compressed logs an error for every frame while subscribed (32FC1 is not JPEG/PNG), so only compressedDepth is enabled on depth. The sim test checks every image, its one transport and its camera_info, each in its camera's optical frame.
    • Mono images are linear: the same scene averaged 120.8 grey in the colour image and 55.6 in the left L8 image. ign-rendering renders every camera into an sRGB texture and decodes it to linear when it makes the L8 image. The sim's mono images are darker than the real OAK's; this is not corrected.
    • No CSI distortion: Fortress's ogre2 does not render lens distortion (ImageBrownDistortionModel is not supported in ogre2), so the sim's CSI sensor has none and its camera_info says D = 0, which matches its pinhole images.
    • Mapping (navigation#6): its launch test runs once per input in the flat world with the frame test's target 3 m ahead and delete_db_on_start:=true over an unreadable old file at database_path. Both inputs pass: the file is replaced by a new SQLite database (without -d rtabmap stops on it with DB error: file is not a database), the standing robot gets a node in map, a 0.6 m drive moves the graph's last node at least 0.4 m ahead with under 0.1 m lateral, map → odom stays at identity on the straight and has no other publisher (nothing on /tf_static), and /map has no occupied cell on the floor in front of the target and every row across its face occupied. With rtabmap's default grid, depth put 56 occupied cells on that floor. A scripted 4 m drive in citysim_block gave 10 graph nodes for both inputs; depth had no occupied cell in the lane, stereo 39 in clusters 4–7 m ahead (the plain road gives stereo few points). On a straight without loop closures map → odom stays at identity anyway, so the test catches a correction, not a frame error that causes none; a camera frame error shows in the /map check instead.
    • citysim on Fortress: the full city loads 638 models with no SDF errors once 12 Classic-only elements and Fortress crashes are worked around (the spike's table; among them <road> not rendered, model:// dependencies the Fuel client skips, collision meshes that segfault DART, actor skins without an animation that crash the sensors' render thread, and zero-length actor trajectories that stall it). The port found three more: buildings coloured by OGRE material scripts rendered black, the jersey barriers had no mesh or collision, and the RoboCup field's infinite collision plane raised the whole city's ground by 6 cm.

    Interaction with the simulation (ADR 0012)

    Everything above goes through ADR 0012's launch path: sim.launch.py only adds the image bridge, the relay and the world, and the stack runs with hardware:=gazebo use_sim_time:=true. perception.launch.py, local_costmap.launch.py and mapping.launch.py (navigation#6) are started next to it with use_sim_time:=true, the same way they will be started on the robot. Which machine runs them on the robot is decided at the robot test.

    citysim's worlds and plugins

    • Two worlds: citysim is the full city (sun shadows off). citysim_block is the city cropped to x −60…30, y −30…30 (86 models, no moving cars). With all cameras the spike measured a real-time factor of 0.199 for the full city and 0.23 for the block, against a target of 0.2. These are the spike's setup: the CSI image was 848×480 (now 1280×720), Bloom and the moving cars did not exist yet (citysim#3 estimates about 2 % for Bloom from one camera, citysim#4 measured about 1.5 % for the cars, server only), and the block's 0.23 ran at a 1 ms physics step, while the shipped block uses 2 ms. On the desktop under other load, the port's drive check got 0.25–0.5 in the block and 0.16–0.22 in the full city, and the stall investigation (pilsbot_simulation#15, load average 6–9 on 4 cores) median 0.17 in the full city with cameras, 0.23 without, and 0.11 in the block: on a loaded desktop the load sets the real-time factor more than the world does. Odometry matched ground truth within 0.5 % in both worlds (full city and block: 10.84 m odometry, 10.88 m ground truth for a 12 s straight). The block stays the world for perception and mapping, because on the unloaded laptop it was the one that met the 0.2 target. Primitive collision proxies for the static scenery are the next lever, built only if the full city's speed hurts.
    • World source: the .sdf files are the source. Classic's Ruby template and .world are deleted; the spike's converter stays in git history. Physics step 2 ms, all scenery static, the heightmap's visual replaced by a mesh of the same heightmap.
    • Fuel: the 54 external models are Fuel URIs. The first launch downloads about 110 MB to ~/.ignition/fuel (about two minutes); no copies are in the repo.
    • TrafficLights: a world system (citysim::TrafficLights) with Classic's <key value model color> config. It listens on /keyboard/keypress and changes the stop lights' visuals, which the sensor cameras see. Keys come from the GUI's KeyPublisher, which replaces Classic's Keyboard plugin; it sends Qt key codes, and the system accepts those and Classic's ASCII codes.
    • Bloom: an ogre2 render pass with Classic's shader chain, added to every colour sensor camera by a world system and to the 3D view by a GUI plugin. Turning it off means removing both plugin elements from the world.
    • Moving cars: the full city's 8 cars follow Classic's trajectories through a kinematic model system, citysim::WaypointMover. Their collisions follow at 10 Hz, and they pass through whatever stands in their way.
    • LensFlare: deferred to Harmonic (pilsbot_simulation#10). Harmonic ships a lens flare pass and system, so a Fortress back-port (3–5 days) would be thrown away on the upgrade.
    • Joy: dropped. The robot is driven through ROS joy_node and teleop_acker_joy, in the sim as on the robot; a Gazebo joystick plugin would publish to a topic nothing reads.

    Considered options

    • Bridge Gazebo's own point cloud: rejected. Its header names a frame its data is not in, the robot has no such cloud, and fixing it would need a sim-only frame. Two pipelines that run unchanged on the robot are what the sim has to test.
    • One point cloud pipeline now: rejected. On-device depth costs the Nano nothing but its quality on the real OAK is unknown; stereo on the host costs CPU and needs texture. The comparison needs the real OAK and its camera_info. One output topic keeps the consumers (the costmaps) the same either way; mapping reads the images and makes the same choice with its own input:=.
    • Images on parameter_bridge: rejected. It gives raw images only, while the robot publishes compressed ones, which Foxglove and the bags use. image_bridge costs the same real-time factor.
    • Use Gazebo's camera_info as published: rejected. Stereo depth comes out at half the distance. The relay is sim-only and takes the baseline from the URDF, so it does not hide a robot fault: on the robot the baseline comes from the OAK's own calibration.
    • CSI images in front_camera_link: the spec's first choice, superseded after the spike. An image stamped with an x-forward link frame is projected rotated by everything that uses it (people tracking later), the same mistake as Gazebo's cloud.
    • Render the CSI distortion, or keep the robot's D in the sim's camera_info: impossible and rejected. A camera_info that claims distortion over a pinhole image misleads every consumer.
    • CSI intrinsics from jetson_camera's raspicam_v2.yaml: rejected after checking what the robot loads. The robot passes no calibration URL, so jetson_camera loads ~/.ros/camera_info/camera.yaml, which is the Pilsbot's own calibration (jetson_camera/camera_info/camera.yaml, 2020). raspicam_v2.yaml is upstream's sample. The sim uses camera.yaml scaled to 1280×720: HFOV 0.71489 rad.
    • Generate the city from the Classic template: rejected. Almost every helper in it emits Classic constructs that changed, and it picked the parked cars at random on every run.
    • Only the full city: rejected. It misses the 0.2 target with all cameras; the block passes it.

    Consequences

    • Milestone 2 is perception: cameras, citysim, point clouds, costmaps, and mapping (navigation#6). Full Nav2 navigation, people tracking with the CSI camera (the sim already has the walking and standing people), GNSS and hardware:=kinematic are later milestones.
    • Merge order: description#11, hw_ros#20, bringup#12, #15 and #16, simulation#12 and #14, citysim#1–#4 and navigation#4, #5 and #6 are merged; this ADR is last. depthai#5 waits for the OAK test.
    • The sim's cameras still differ from the robot's (ADR 0012 lists the rest): mono images are linear and raw, the CSI camera_info has D = 0, fx = fy and the principal point at the image centre (the robot's: 606.6, 391.2), depth has only compressedDepth (the robot publishes no compressed depth), and the CSI camera offers only /compressed while the robot's jetson_camera publishes every installed transport. /camera/stereo/points no longer exists.
    • On the robot, mjpeg_node publishes the mono pair as JPEG only (image_rect/compressed), so whatever reads a raw mono image needs an image_transport republish compressed raw for that camera: cloud:=stereo and mapping.launch.py input:=stereo for both, input:=depth for the left one (both mapping inputs read the left image raw; without it rtabmap builds no map), cloud:=depth for none. cloud:=depth and input:=depth need mjpeg_node with enable_depth:=true. All of them need depthai#5.
    • Not verified on the robot: that the Nano has ~/.ros/camera_info/camera.yaml (without it the CSI camera_info is uncalibrated), and that camera.yaml, calibrated at 1080p, holds for the 1280×720 capture mode.
    • New bags record /front_camera/image_raw/compressed and /front_camera/camera_info. While the camera container still runs Foxy, its CSI camera publishes /image_raw/compressed and /camera_info, so those bags have no CSI stream (ADR 0010).
    • The sim's CSI camera now renders 1280×720 instead of 848×480, and Bloom and the moving cars were added after the spike. Its real-time factors with the shipped worlds are measured only on the loaded desktop (pilsbot_simulation#15), not on an unloaded machine (citysim's worlds and plugins, above).
    • stereo needs texture: untextured surfaces give no points. The flat world's ground and the city's roads and sidewalks are plain colours, so the stereo cloud has no ground points there. The stereo cloud also misses a band at the left image border (+y), and the frame test target is centred for that reason.
    • depth_image_proc publishes best effort and stereo_image_proc reliable: a reliable subscriber works with stereo and silently gets nothing with depth. Tests and the costmaps (Nav2's obstacle layer) subscribe with sensor-data QoS.
    • The costmap's minimum obstacle height is 0.1 m (the floor the camera sees from x ≈ 1.45 m on would otherwise be lethal), the local window is 8 × 8 m so that it holds the camera's marking range (the OAK at x 0.82 plus obstacle_range 3 m; the old 3 × 3 m ended at 1.5 m, where the OAK's view of the floor only begins), and the footprint is the robot with the steering straight ahead. The standalone costmap's topics are /costmap/costmap* (in a Nav2 bringup: /local_costmap/local_costmap*). All three are to be tuned on the robot.
    • Fortress exits with code 0 on SDF errors, and the actor crash and stall appear only once a camera renders. citysim's load test therefore checks that /stats advances with a subscribed camera in the world, not the exit code.
    • citysim's plugins find ignition-gazebo6, whose jsoncppConfig.cmake fails on CMake 4 (cmake_policy(VERSION 3.0)). On a desktop with CMake 4 build citysim with -DCMAKE_POLICY_VERSION_MINIMUM=3.5 (citysim's README).
    • Resolved: the full city drives on the desktop (pilsbot_simulation#15; citysim#1 had it as "Full city: not confirmed"). The earlier failure (4.4 m instead of about 12 m in a 12 s straight at 1.0 m/s, no /odom in the following 6 s turn) came from the ad-hoc drive script, not from the robot stack or the world. Before every phase the script read ground truth with a blocking ign model -m pilsbot -p, which takes 39 s wall time, about 2 GB RAM and a full core in the full city (1–2 s in the block). rclpy did not spin meanwhile, so the node's /clock time stood still, and the next phase was timed from that old time: about 6 s of sim time were gone per query, half the straight and the whole turn. The reported 23 Hz /odom was the straight's messages divided by 12 s, though they arrived in about 6 s. A separate monitor showed the stack fine throughout: controllers and PilsbotSystem active, the hardware activator never re-activating, list_controllers answering in 0.01 s or less, /odom at 50.0 messages per sim second (the controllers' 50 Hz) and /joint_states gaps of at most 0.020 s sim. With ground truth read from one dynamic_pose/info message in a thread while the node spins, the full city drives the 12 s straight like the block (10.84 m odometry, 10.88 m ground truth) at a median real-time factor of 0.17 with cameras and 0.23 with cameras:=false (same distances), on the 4-core desktop at load average 6–9 with full swap. Load lowers the real-time factor but does not stop the robot. No code changes followed; pilsbot_simulation's citysim test drives until odometry reaches its distance, in the block, and is not affected. The Gazebo GUI and driving with the gamepad in the full city were not part of the check.
    • Right turn from the city spawn point: in both city worlds a right turn (steering +0.3 from y = −1.8) ends with ground-truth yaw −63° against −23° from odometry and 2.3 m against 3.55 m travelled, while a left turn in the block and a right turn on flat ground match. Most likely the robot runs into the right-hand curb and pivots there while the wheels turn; this is not checked with a contact sensor. It is the scenario, not a stall: /odom keeps arriving at 50 Hz.
    • The Bloom GUI plugin and the TrafficLights keys from the KeyPublisher window are not verified: everything ran headless.
    • Fortress logs texture lookups inside some Fuel models (house normal maps, wheel textures of the buses and hatchbacks); those textures are missing. citysim's README lists them.
    • pilsbot_navigation's Gazebo tests (frame test, costmap test, mapping test) start the sim and take minutes; -LE gazebo or PILSBOT_SKIP_GAZEBO_TESTS=1 skips them.
